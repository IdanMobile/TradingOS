# Experiments, memory, and continuous improvement

## Experiment record

Every material research experiment should record:

- hypothesis;
- expected mechanism;
- strategy and asset scope;
- timeframe and market regime;
- dataset and feature versions;
- parameters and changes;
- benchmark and control;
- cost and execution assumptions;
- results and uncertainty;
- failure modes;
- decision: reject, revise, paper, or promote;
- owner and date.

## Separate scores

Do not collapse everything into one confidence score. Track separately:

- strategy evidence score;
- data-quality score;
- model score;
- agent/reasoning score;
- economic net-expectancy score;
- execution-feasibility score;
- portfolio-fit score;
- operational and security score.

The supervisor may produce a final decision, but preserve the component scores and rationale.

## Durable project memory

Store only useful, reviewable memory:

- locked decisions;
- verified architecture facts;
- known limitations;
- rejected strategies and reasons;
- failed experiments;
- source and dataset lineage;
- recurring implementation mistakes;
- current open questions;
- last reviewed commit or version.

Do not store secrets, raw credentials, unverified rumors, or unexplained conclusions.

## Learning loop

1. Capture the outcome.
2. Compare it with the original hypothesis and forecast.
3. Classify the error: data, logic, execution, regime, risk, or process.
4. Decide whether the lesson generalizes.
5. Update the relevant reference or project decision record.
6. Do not change a strategy from a tiny sample or a single loss.
